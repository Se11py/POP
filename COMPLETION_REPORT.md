## 💡 WHAT MAKES THIS COMPREHENSIVE

Traditional Glossaries:
- "Friction: Force opposing motion"

This Glossary (Example Entry):

**Definition**: Force arising from contact surfaces resisting relative motion; proportional to normal force but independent of contact area; kinetic friction constant during motion; static friction increases until motion begins.

**Methodology**: Measured via force sensors, friction blocks on inclined planes, comparison of kinetic vs. static coefficients

**Mathematical Model**: 
- F_friction = μ × N (kinetic or static)
- μ typically 0-1 (unitless)
- F_static_max = μ_s × N
- F_kinetic = μ_k × N (where μ_k < μ_s)

**Applications**: 
- Braking systems (tires on road)
- Bearings (friction reduction)
- Climbing (friction enables grip)
- Conveyor belts (friction provides traction)

**Examiner Insight**: 
- Students often think friction proportional to speed (incorrect)
- Mark schemes expect coefficient definition
- Common error: confusing static vs. kinetic

**Relational Schema**:
- Component of Newton's Laws
- Opposite to Applied Force (net force = applied - friction)
- Related to Normal Force (perpendicular contact)
- Prerequisites: Force, Contact, Normal reaction
