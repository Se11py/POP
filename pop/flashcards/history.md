# IGCSE & A Level History Flashcards

## [METHODOLOGY] ANALYSIS & SKILLS
- **Front**: Primary Source
  **Back**: A document or physical object created during the specific time period under study.
- **Front**: Secondary Source
  **Back**: A document or recording created after an event, relating or discussing information originally presented elsewhere.
- **Front**: Historiography
  **Back**: The study of how historical interpretations differ and evolve over time.
- **Front**: Provenance
  **Back**: The origin of a source (Who, When, Where, Why) used to evaluate its reliability and utility.
- **Front**: Reliability
  **Back**: The degree to which a source can be trusted to provide accurate factual information.
- **Front**: Utility
  **Back**: The usefulness of a source in helping to answer a specific historical question (a source can be useful even if biased).
- **Front**: Revisionist View
  **Back**: A historical interpretation that challenges and seeks to change the established orthodox view of an event.

## [IGCSE] THE TREATY OF VERSAILLES (1919)
- **Front**: Article 231
  **Back**: The "War Guilt Clause" forcing Germany to accept total responsibility for WWI.
- **Front**: Reparations
  **Back**: Financial compensation imposed on Germany, set at £6.6 billion in 1921.
- **Front**: Diktat
  **Back**: The German term for the Treaty of Versailles, meaning "dictated peace," as they were not allowed to negotiate.
- **Front**: Demilitarisation of the Rhineland
  **Back**: A term of the Treaty ensuring no German troops or weapons were allowed in this border region with France.
- **Front**: The Big Three
  **Back**: Woodrow Wilson (USA), Georges Clemenceau (France), David Lloyd George (Britain).

## [IGCSE] THE LEAGUE OF NATIONS (1920s-30s)
- **Front**: Collective Security
  **Back**: The principle that an attack on one member of the League is an attack on all, requiring a joint response.
- **Front**: Economic Sanctions
  **Back**: Stopping trade with an aggressor nation to pressure them into stopping a conflict.
- **Front**: The Aaland Islands (1921)
  **Back**: A success for the League where it successfully mediated a territorial dispute between Sweden and Finland.
- **Front**: The Manchurian Crisis (1931)
  **Back**: A major failure where the League failed to stop Japan's invasion of Chinese territory.
- **Front**: The Abyssinian Crisis (1935)
  **Back**: The crisis that effectively "killed" the League after it failed to stop Italy's invasion of Abyssinia (Ethiopia).

## [IGCSE] THE ORIGINS OF WWII
- **Front**: Appeasement
  **Back**: The policy of making concessions to a dictatorial power to avoid conflict (primarily associated with Neville Chamberlain).
- **Front**: Anschluss (1938)
  **Back**: The political union of Germany and Austria, forbidden by the Treaty of Versailles but achieved by Hitler.
- **Front**: The Munich Agreement (1938)
  **Back**: A settlement permitting Nazi Germany's annexation of the Sudetenland in Czechoslovakia.
- **Front**: Nazi-Soviet Pact (1939)
  **Back**: A non-aggression agreement between Hitler and Stalin to divide Poland between them.

## [A LEVEL] THE COLD WAR (1945-1991)
- **Front**: The Truman Doctrine (1947)
  **Back**: US policy to provide military and economic aid to any nation threatened by communist takeover (Containment).
- **Front**: The Marshall Plan (1948)
  **Back**: A massive US economic aid package to rebuild Western Europe and prevent the spread of communism.
- **Front**: NATO (1949)
  **Back**: The North Atlantic Treaty Organization; a Western military alliance for mutual defense against the USSR.
- **Front**: The Warsaw Pact (1955)
  **Back**: A collective defense treaty signed among the Soviet Union and seven Soviet satellite states.
- **Front**: Proxy War
  **Back**: A conflict where third parties fight on behalf of more powerful nations (e.g., Vietnam War, Korean War).
- **Front**: Détente
  **Back**: A period of improved relations and eased tensions between the US and the Soviet Union in the 1970s.
- **Front**: Glasnost
  **Back**: Mikhail Gorbachev's policy of "openness" in the Soviet government and society.
- **Front**: Perestroika
  **Back**: Mikhail Gorbachev's policy of "restructuring" the Soviet economy and political system.
