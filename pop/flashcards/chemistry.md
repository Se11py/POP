# Chemistry Flashcards

## [IGCSE] C1: THE PARTICULATE NATURE OF MATTER
- **Front**: Solid
  **Back**: State with fixed shape/volume; particles vibrate in fixed positions.
- **Front**: Liquid
  **Back**: State with fixed volume but assumes shape of container; particles slide over each other.
- **Front**: Gas
  **Back**: State with no fixed shape/volume; particles move rapidly and randomly.
- **Front**: Sublimation
  **Back**: The direct change of state from a solid to a gas.
- **Front**: Melting
  **Back**: The change from solid to liquid.
- **Front**: Boiling
  **Back**: The change from liquid to gas at a specific temperature.
- **Front**: Evaporation
  **Back**: The change from liquid to gas at any temperature (occurs at the surface).
- **Front**: Condensation
  **Back**: The change from gas to liquid.
- **Front**: Freezing
  **Back**: The change from liquid to solid.
- **Front**: Diffusion in Gases
  **Back**: The random movement of particles from high to low concentration; lower molecular mass gases diffuse faster.

## [IGCSE] C2: EXPERIMENTAL TECHNIQUES
- **Front**: Solute
  **Back**: A substance that is dissolved in a solvent.
- **Front**: Solvent
  **Back**: A substance that dissolves a solute.
- **Front**: Solution
  **Back**: A mixture of a solute and a solvent.
- **Front**: Saturated Solution
  **Back**: A solution containing the maximum amount of solute that can be dissolved at a given temperature.
- **Front**: Filtration
  **Back**: Separating an insoluble solid from a liquid.
- **Front**: Distillation
  **Back**: Separating a liquid from a solution (e.g., water from salt water).
- **Front**: Fractional Distillation
  **Back**: Separating a mixture of liquids with different boiling points.
- **Front**: Chromatography
  **Back**: Separating a mixture of soluble substances.
- **Front**: Rf Value
  **Back**: Distance moved by substance / Distance moved by solvent front.

## [IGCSE] C3: ATOMS, ELEMENTS AND COMPOUNDS
- **Front**: Element
  **Back**: A substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.
- **Front**: Compound
  **Back**: A substance made of two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions.
- **Front**: Mixture
  **Back**: Two or more substances mixed together but not chemically combined.
- **Front**: Proton Number (Atomic Number)
  **Back**: The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.
- **Front**: Nucleon Number (Mass Number)
  **Back**: The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
- **Front**: Isotopes
  **Back**: Atoms of the same element with the same proton number but a different nucleon number.
- **Front**: Noble Gases
  **Back**: Group VIII elements; unreactive because they have a full outer shell of electrons.
- **Front**: Ionic Bond
  **Back**: Strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
- **Front**: Covalent Bond
  **Back**: Bond formed by the sharing of pairs of electrons between atoms.
- **Front**: Giant Covalent Structure
  **Back**: A 3D network of atoms held together by strong covalent bonds (e.g., diamond, graphite).
- **Front**: Metallic Bond
  **Back**: The electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and a sea of delocalised electrons.

## [IGCSE] C4: STOICHIOMETRY
- **Front**: Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)
  **Back**: The average mass of naturally occurring atoms of an element on a scale where the C-12 atom has a mass of exactly 12 units.
- **Front**: Relative Molecular Mass (Mr)
  **Back**: The sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in a molecule.
- **Front**: The Mole
  **Back**: The amount of substance that contains 6.02 x 10^23 particles.
- **Front**: Molar Volume of a Gas
  **Back**: 24 dm^3 at room temperature and pressure (rtp).

## [IGCSE] C5: ELECTRICITY AND CHEMISTRY
- **Front**: Electrolysis
  **Back**: The breakdown of an ionic compound, molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of electricity.
- **Front**: Anode
  **Back**: The positive electrode where oxidation occurs.
- **Front**: Cathode
  **Back**: The negative electrode where reduction occurs.
- **Front**: Electroplating
  **Back**: Using electrolysis to coat the surface of one metal with another metal.

## [IGCSE] C6: ENERGY CHANGES IN CHEMICAL REACTIONS
- **Front**: Exothermic Reaction
  **Back**: A reaction that releases thermal energy to the surroundings (ΔH is negative).
- **Front**: Endothermic Reaction
  **Back**: A reaction that absorbs thermal energy from the surroundings (ΔH is positive).
- **Front**: Activation Energy
  **Back**: The minimum energy that colliding particles must have to react.

## [IGCSE] C7: CHEMICAL REACTIONS
- **Front**: Collision Theory
  **Back**: For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy and the correct orientation.
- **Front**: Redox
  **Back**: A reaction involving both oxidation and reduction.
- **Front**: Oxidation (Electrons)
  **Back**: Loss of electrons (OIL).
- **Front**: Reduction (Electrons)
  **Back**: Gain of electrons (RIG).
- **Front**: Reducing Agent
  **Back**: A substance that removes oxygen or adds electrons to another substance.

## [IGCSE] C8: ACIDS, BASES AND SALTS
- **Front**: pH of Acid
  **Back**: Less than 7.
- **Front**: pH of Base/Alkali
  **Back**: Greater than 7.
- **Front**: Acid + Metal
  **Back**: Salt + Hydrogen gas.
- **Front**: Acid + Base
  **Back**: Salt + Water (Neutralisation).
- **Front**: Acid + Carbonate
  **Back**: Salt + Water + Carbon Dioxide gas.

## [A LEVEL] ATOMIC STRUCTURE
- **Front**: First Ionisation Energy
  **Back**: The energy required to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions.
- **Front**: Orbitals (s, p, d, f)
  **Back**: Regions of space where there is a high probability of finding an electron. s-orbitals are spherical; p-orbitals are dumbbell-shaped.
- **Front**: Electron Configuration of Copper (Cu)
  **Back**: [Ar] 3d10 4s1 (an exception to the expected rule).
- **Front**: Electron Configuration of Chromium (Cr)
  **Back**: [Ar] 3d5 4s1 (an exception to the expected rule).

## [A LEVEL] CHEMICAL BONDING
- **Front**: Electronegativity
  **Back**: The power of an atom to attract the pair of electrons in a covalent bond.
- **Front**: Hydrogen Bonding
  **Back**: Strongest intermolecular force; occurs between H and N, O, or F.
- **Front**: Bond Polarity
  **Back**: The result of an unequal sharing of electrons between atoms with different electronegativities.
- **Front**: Giant Metallic Lattice
  **Back**: A regular 3D arrangement of positive metal ions surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons.

## [A LEVEL] ENTHALPY CHANGES
- **Front**: Standard Enthalpy Change of Formation (ΔHf°)
  **Back**: Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions.
- **Front**: Standard Enthalpy Change of Combustion (ΔHc°)
  **Back**: Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a substance is burnt completely in excess oxygen under standard conditions.
- **Front**: Hess's Law
  **Back**: The enthalpy change for a chemical reaction is independent of the route taken.
- **Front**: Average Bond Enthalpy
  **Back**: The energy needed to break one mole of a specific type of bond in a gaseous molecule, averaged over many compounds.

## [A LEVEL] EQUILIBRIA
- **Front**: Le Chatelier's Principle
  **Back**: If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, the equilibrium position moves in the direction that tends to reduce that disturbance.
- **Front**: Kc (Equilibrium Constant)
  **Back**: [Products] / [Reactants], each raised to the power of their stoichiometric coefficients. Only affected by temperature.
- **Front**: Haber Process Conditions
  **Back**: 450°C, 200 atm, Iron catalyst.

## [A LEVEL] REACTION KINETICS
- **Front**: Rate Equation
  **Back**: Rate = k [A]^m [B]^n, where k is the rate constant and m, n are the orders of reaction.
- **Front**: Half-life (t1/2)
  **Back**: The time taken for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half of its initial value. For first-order reactions, it is constant.
- **Front**: Catalyst Effect on Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution
  **Back**: Provides an alternative route with a lower activation energy; a higher proportion of particles have energy greater than the activation energy.

## [A LEVEL] ORGANIC CHEMISTRY FUNDAMENTALS
- **Front**: Homologous Series
  **Back**: A family of organic compounds with the same functional group and general formula, where each member differs by a CH2 group.
- **Front**: Structural Isomers
  **Back**: Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulas.
- **Front**: Stereoisomers
  **Back**: Compounds with the same structural formula but a different arrangement of atoms in space (e.g., E/Z or optical isomers).
- **Front**: Chiral Centre
  **Back**: An atom (usually carbon) bonded to four different groups, resulting in optical isomerism.
- **Front**: Nucleophile
  **Back**: An electron-pair donor (e.g., OH-, NH3, CN-).
- **Front**: Electrophile
  **Back**: An electron-pair acceptor (e.g., Br2, HBr, NO2+).
