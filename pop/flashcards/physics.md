# Physics Flashcards

## [IGCSE] P1: MOTION, FORCES AND ENERGY

### 1.1 Physical Quantities
- **Front**: Scalar Quantity
  **Back**: A quantity that has magnitude only (e.g., speed, distance, mass, energy).
- **Front**: Vector Quantity
  **Back**: A quantity that has both magnitude and direction (e.g., velocity, displacement, force, acceleration).

### 1.2 Motion
- **Front**: Speed Formula
  **Back**: Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time.
- **Front**: Velocity
  **Back**: Speed in a specific direction.
- **Front**: Acceleration Formula
  **Back**: a = (v - u) / t.
- **Front**: Gradient of Distance-Time Graph
  **Back**: Represents the speed of the object.
- **Front**: Gradient of Velocity-Time Graph
  **Back**: Represents the acceleration of the object.
- **Front**: Area under Velocity-Time Graph
  **Back**: Represents the total distance travelled (or displacement).
- **Front**: Acceleration of Free Fall (g)
  **Back**: Constant acceleration near Earth's surface, approximately 9.8 m/s².
- **Front**: Terminal Velocity
  **Back**: The constant speed reached when weight equals the upward force of air resistance.

### 1.3 Mass, Weight and Density
- **Front**: Mass
  **Back**: A measure of the amount of matter in an object; it resists change in motion.
- **Front**: Weight Formula
  **Back**: W = m * g.
- **Front**: Density Formula
  **Back**: ρ = m / V.

### 1.4 Forces
- **Front**: Hooke's Law
  **Back**: Load is proportional to extension (F = kx), provided the limit of proportionality is not exceeded.
- **Front**: Newton's Second Law
  **Back**: Force = mass * acceleration (F = ma).
- **Front**: Friction
  **Back**: The force between two surfaces that may impede motion and produce heating.
- **Front**: Centripetal Force
  **Back**: The resultant force acting towards the centre of a circle for an object in circular motion.
- **Front**: Moment of a Force
  **Back**: A measure of its turning effect; Moment = Force * perpendicular distance from the pivot.
- **Front**: Principle of Moments
  **Back**: For an object in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments equals the sum of anticlockwise moments.

### 1.5 Work, Energy and Power
- **Front**: Principle of Conservation of Energy
  **Back**: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one form to another.
- **Front**: Kinetic Energy Formula
  **Back**: KE = 1/2 * m * v².
- **Front**: Gravitational Potential Energy Formula
  **Back**: ΔGPE = m * g * Δh.
- **Front**: Work Done Formula
  **Back**: W = F * d (force * distance moved in direction of force).
- **Front**: Power Formula
  **Back**: P = ΔE / t or P = W / t.
- **Front**: Efficiency Formula
  **Back**: (Useful Output / Total Input) * 100%.

## [IGCSE] P2: THERMAL PHYSICS
- **Front**: Specific Heat Capacity (c)
  **Back**: The energy required to raise 1kg of a substance by 1°C (ΔE = mcΔθ).
- **Front**: Evaporation vs Boiling
  **Back**: Evaporation: occurs at surface, any temperature below boiling point. Boiling: occurs throughout liquid, at specific temperature.
- **Front**: Thermal Conduction
  **Back**: Transfer of thermal energy through molecular vibrations and (in metals) delocalised electrons.
- **Front**: Convection
  **Back**: Transfer of thermal energy in fluids through density changes and current flow.
- **Front**: Thermal Radiation
  **Back**: Infrared radiation that does not require a medium to travel.

## [A LEVEL] PARTICLES AND RADIATION
- **Front**: Specific Charge
  **Back**: The ratio of a particle's charge to its mass (C/kg).
- **Front**: Strong Nuclear Force Range
  **Back**: Attractive between 0.5 and 3.0 fm; repulsive below 0.5 fm.
- **Front**: Alpha Decay
  **Back**: Emission of a helium nucleus (2 protons, 2 neutrons); mass number -4, atomic number -2.
- **Front**: Beta Minus Decay
  **Back**: A neutron turns into a proton, an electron, and an anti-electron neutrino.
- **Front**: Antiparticle
  **Back**: A particle with the same rest mass but opposite charge and other quantum numbers.
- **Front**: Annihilation
  **Back**: When a particle and its antiparticle meet, they destroy each other and release two photons.
- **Front**: Pair Production
  **Back**: A single photon turns into a particle and its corresponding antiparticle.

## [A LEVEL] QUANTUM PHENOMENA
- **Front**: Photoelectric Effect
  **Back**: The emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of a high enough frequency shines on it.
- **Front**: Work Function (Φ)
  **Back**: The minimum energy required for an electron to escape from the surface of a metal.
- **Front**: Threshold Frequency (f0)
  **Back**: The minimum frequency of light required to cause photoelectric emission.
- **Front**: Einstein's Photoelectric Equation
  **Back**: hf = Φ + Ek(max).
- **Front**: Line Spectrum Evidence
  **Back**: Provides evidence for discrete energy levels in atoms.
- **Front**: de Broglie Wavelength
  **Back**: λ = h / mv (relates wavelength to momentum).

## [A LEVEL] FIELDS
- **Front**: Electric Field Strength (E)
  **Back**: The force per unit positive charge acting on a small test charge at a point (E = F/q).
- **Front**: Coulomb's Law
  **Back**: The force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.
- **Front**: Magnetic Flux Density (B)
  **Back**: A measure of the strength of a magnetic field; F = BIL sinθ.
- **Front**: Faraday's Law
  **Back**: The induced e.m.f. is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.
- **Front**: Lenz's Law
  **Back**: The direction of an induced e.m.f. is such that it opposes the change that created it.
