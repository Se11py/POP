# Biology Flashcards

## [IGCSE] B1: CHARACTERISTICS OF LIVING ORGANISMS
- **Front**: Movement
  **Back**: An action by an organism or part of an organism causing a change of position or place.
- **Front**: Respiration
  **Back**: The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism.
- **Front**: Sensitivity
  **Back**: The ability to detect or sense stimuli in the internal or external environment and to make appropriate responses.
- **Front**: Growth
  **Back**: A permanent increase in size and dry mass by an increase in cell number or cell size or both.
- **Front**: Reproduction
  **Back**: The processes that make more of the same kind of organism.
- **Front**: Excretion
  **Back**: Removal from organisms of the waste products of metabolism, toxic materials, and substances in excess of requirements.
- **Front**: Nutrition
  **Back**: Taking in of materials for energy, growth and development.

## [IGCSE] B2: CELLS
- **Front**: Cell Membrane
  **Back**: A partially permeable barrier that controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
- **Front**: Cell Wall
  **Back**: A rigid external layer of a plant cell, made of cellulose, providing structural support and protection.
- **Front**: Cytoplasm
  **Back**: The jelly-like substance inside a cell where most chemical reactions take place.
- **Front**: Nucleus
  **Back**: An organelle containing genetic material (DNA) that controls the cell's activities.
- **Front**: Chloroplast
  **Back**: An organelle found in plant cells that contains chlorophyll and is the site of photosynthesis.
- **Front**: Chlorophyll
  **Back**: A green pigment found in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy used to carry out photosynthesis.
- **Front**: Vacuole
  **Back**: A fluid-filled sac used for the storage of materials inside a cell; plant cells have a large permanent central vacuole containing cell sap.
- **Front**: Mitochondria
  **Back**: Organelles that carry out aerobic respiration to release energy for the cell.
- **Front**: Ribosomes
  **Back**: Small organelles where protein synthesis occurs.
- **Front**: Magnification Formula
  **Back**: Magnification = Image Size / Actual Size (M = I/A).
- **Front**: Ciliated Cells
  **Back**: Specialized cells found in the trachea and bronchi that move mucus upwards.
- **Front**: Root Hair Cells
  **Back**: Specialized cells in plant roots with a large surface area for the absorption of water and minerals.
- **Front**: Xylem Vessels
  **Back**: Hollow tubes in plants that transport water and minerals and provide structural support.
- **Front**: Palisade Mesophyll Cells
  **Back**: Tightly packed cells in leaves containing many chloroplasts for maximum photosynthesis.
- **Front**: Red Blood Cells
  **Back**: Biconcave cells lacking a nucleus, containing haemoglobin for oxygen transport.
- **Front**: Sperm and Egg Cells
  **Back**: Gametes specialized for reproduction, containing half the genetic material (haploid).

## [IGCSE] B3: MOVEMENT IN AND OUT OF CELLS
- **Front**: Diffusion
  **Back**: The net movement of particles from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement.
- **Front**: Osmosis
  **Back**: The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution), through a partially permeable membrane.
- **Front**: Active Transport
  **Back**: The movement of particles through a cell membrane from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration using energy from respiration.
- **Front**: Turgid
  **Back**: A plant cell that has absorbed water and has cytoplasm that is pressing outwards on the cell wall.
- **Front**: Plasmolysis
  **Back**: The condition in which a plant cell has lost water by osmosis and its cytoplasm has shrunk away from the cell wall.
- **Front**: Flaccid
  **Back**: A cell that has lost water and is no longer firm.

## [IGCSE] B4: BIOLOGICAL MOLECULES
- **Front**: Elements in Carbohydrates
  **Back**: Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen.
- **Front**: Elements in Fats
  **Back**: Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen.
- **Front**: Elements in Proteins
  **Back**: Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, and sometimes Sulfur.
- **Front**: Monomers of Starch/Glycogen
  **Back**: Glucose.
- **Front**: Monomers of Proteins
  **Back**: Amino acids.
- **Front**: Monomers of Fats/Lipids
  **Back**: Fatty acids and Glycerol.
- **Front**: Iodine Test
  **Back**: Tests for Starch; turns from orange-brown to blue-black.
- **Front**: Benedict's Test
  **Back**: Tests for Reducing Sugars; turns from blue to green/yellow/orange/brick-red upon heating.
- **Front**: Biuret Test
  **Back**: Tests for Proteins; turns from blue to violet/purple.
- **Front**: Ethanol Emulsion Test
  **Back**: Tests for Fats/Lipids; forms a cloudy white emulsion.
- **Front**: DCPIP Test
  **Back**: Tests for Vitamin C; turns from blue to colorless.
- **Front**: DNA Structure
  **Back**: Two strands coiled together to form a double helix, with cross-links between pairs of bases (A-T and C-G).

## [IGCSE] B5: ENZYMES
- **Front**: Enzyme
  **Back**: A protein that functions as a biological catalyst.
- **Front**: Catalyst
  **Back**: A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction and is not changed by the reaction.
- **Front**: Active Site
  **Back**: The part of an enzyme where the substrate binds.
- **Front**: Denaturation
  **Back**: The loss of the active site's shape, often due to high temperature or extreme pH, preventing the substrate from binding.
- **Front**: Optimum Temperature
  **Back**: The temperature at which an enzyme works fastest.

## [IGCSE] B6: PLANT NUTRITION
- **Front**: Photosynthesis Equation
  **Back**: 6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2 (in the presence of light and chlorophyll).
- **Front**: Limiting Factor
  **Back**: Something present in the environment in such short supply that it restricts life processes (e.g., light intensity, CO2 concentration, temperature for photosynthesis).
- **Front**: Stomata
  **Back**: Small pores on the underside of a leaf through which gases diffuse.
- **Front**: Magnesium Ions in Plants
  **Back**: Required for making chlorophyll; deficiency causes yellowing between veins (chlorosis).
- **Front**: Nitrate Ions in Plants
  **Back**: Required for making amino acids and proteins; deficiency causes stunted growth.

## [IGCSE] B7: HUMAN NUTRITION
- **Front**: Balanced Diet
  **Back**: A diet providing all the required nutrients in the correct proportions.
- **Front**: Malnutrition
  **Back**: An unbalanced diet; can lead to starvation, constipation, coronary heart disease, obesity, or scurvy.
- **Front**: Scurvy Cause
  **Back**: Vitamin C deficiency.
- **Front**: Rickets Cause
  **Back**: Vitamin D deficiency.
- **Front**: Anemia Cause
  **Back**: Iron deficiency.
- **Front**: Kwashiorkor/Marasmus Cause
  **Back**: Protein-energy malnutrition.
- **Front**: Peristalsis
  **Back**: Rhythmical muscular contractions that move food through the alimentary canal.
- **Front**: Ingestion
  **Back**: Taking in substances into the body through the mouth.
- **Front**: Digestion
  **Back**: Breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, water-soluble molecules using mechanical and chemical processes.
- **Front**: Absorption
  **Back**: Movement of digested food molecules through the wall of the intestine into the blood.
- **Front**: Assimilation
  **Back**: Movement of digested food molecules into the cells where they are used, becoming part of the cells.
- **Front**: Egestion
  **Back**: Passing out of food that has not been digested or absorbed, as faeces, through the anus.
- **Front**: Villi
  **Back**: Finger-like projections in the small intestine that increase surface area for absorption.

## [IGCSE] B8: TRANSPORT IN PLANTS
- **Front**: Transpiration
  **Back**: The loss of water vapour from plant leaves by evaporation of water at the surfaces of the mesophyll cells followed by diffusion of water vapour through the stomata.
- **Front**: Translocation
  **Back**: The movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from regions of production (source) to regions of storage or use (sink).

## [IGCSE] B9: TRANSPORT IN ANIMALS
- **Front**: Circulatory System
  **Back**: A system of blood vessels with a pump and valves to ensure one-way flow of blood.
- **Front**: Double Circulation
  **Back**: Blood passes through the heart twice for every complete circuit of the body.
- **Front**: Arteries
  **Back**: Thick-walled vessels that carry blood away from the heart at high pressure.
- **Front**: Veins
  **Back**: Thin-walled vessels with valves that carry blood towards the heart at low pressure.
- **Front**: Capillaries
  **Back**: Tiny vessels with walls one cell thick to allow for the exchange of materials between blood and tissue fluid.
- **Front**: Components of Blood
  **Back**: Red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, and plasma.

## [IGCSE] B10: GAS EXCHANGE AND RESPIRATION
- **Front**: Aerobic Respiration Equation
  **Back**: C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + Energy.
- **Front**: Anaerobic Respiration (Humans)
  **Back**: Glucose -> Lactic Acid + (less) Energy.
- **Front**: Anaerobic Respiration (Yeast)
  **Back**: Glucose -> Alcohol + Carbon Dioxide + (less) Energy.
- **Front**: Alveoli Features
  **Back**: Large surface area, thin walls, good blood supply, and good ventilation for efficient gas exchange.

## [IGCSE] B11: COORDINATION AND RESPONSE
- **Front**: Hormone
  **Back**: A chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs.
- **Front**: Adrenaline Effects
  **Back**: Increased heart rate, increased breathing rate, dilated pupils, and increased blood glucose concentration.
- **Front**: Homeostasis
  **Back**: The maintenance of a constant internal environment.
- **Front**: Negative Feedback
  **Back**: A mechanism where a change in a system triggers a response that reverses the change.
- **Front**: Phototropism
  **Back**: A response in which a plant grows towards or away from direction from which light is coming.
- **Front**: Gravitropism
  **Back**: A response in which a plant grows towards or away from gravity.
- **Front**: Auxin
  **Back**: A plant hormone that controls growth by causing cell elongation.

## [IGCSE] B12: REPRODUCTION
- **Front**: Asexual Reproduction
  **Back**: A process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent.
- **Front**: Sexual Reproduction
  **Back**: A process involving the fusion of the nuclei of two gametes (fertilization) to form a zygote and the production of offspring that are genetically different from each other.
- **Front**: Pollination
  **Back**: Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.

## [IGCSE] B13: INHERITANCE
- **Front**: Chromosome
  **Back**: A thread-like structure of DNA, carrying genetic information in the form of genes.
- **Front**: Gene
  **Back**: A length of DNA that codes for a protein.
- **Front**: Allele
  **Back**: A version of a gene.
- **Front**: Haploid Nucleus
  **Back**: A nucleus containing a single set of unpaired chromosomes (e.g., in gametes).
- **Front**: Diploid Nucleus
  **Back**: A nucleus containing two sets of chromosomes (e.g., in body cells).
- **Front**: Mitosis
  **Back**: Nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells.
- **Front**: Meiosis
  **Back**: Reduction division in which the chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid resulting in genetically different cells.
- **Front**: Genotype
  **Back**: The genetic make-up of an organism in terms of the alleles present.
- **Front**: Phenotype
  **Back**: The observable features of an organism.
- **Front**: Homozygous
  **Back**: Having two identical alleles of a particular gene.
- **Front**: Heterozygous
  **Back**: Having two different alleles of a particular gene.
- **Front**: Dominant
  **Back**: An allele that is expressed if it is present.
- **Front**: Recessive
  **Back**: An allele that is only expressed when there is no dominant allele of the gene present.

## [A LEVEL] CELL STRUCTURE & MICROSCOPY
- **Front**: Magnification
  **Back**: The number of times larger an image is compared with the real size of the object.
- **Front**: Resolution
  **Back**: The ability to distinguish between two separate points; it is limited by the wavelength of radiation used.
- **Front**: Eukaryotic Cell
  **Back**: A cell containing a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., mitochondria, Golgi apparatus).
- **Front**: Prokaryotic Cell
  **Back**: A cell lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; DNA is circular and free in the cytoplasm.
- **Front**: Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER)
  **Back**: A network of flattened sacs (cisternae) studded with ribosomes; the site of protein synthesis and transport.
- **Front**: Golgi Apparatus
  **Back**: A stack of flattened sacs that modifies, sorts, and packages proteins into vesicles.
- **Front**: Lysosome
  **Back**: A vesicle containing hydrolytic (digestive) enzymes used to break down waste or old organelles.
- **Front**: Centrioles
  **Back**: Cylindrical structures made of microtubules, involved in spindle formation during animal cell division.

## [A LEVEL] BIOLOGICAL MOLECULES
- **Front**: Hydrogen Bonding in Water
  **Back**: Weak electrostatic attraction between the slightly positive hydrogen atom of one water molecule and the slightly negative oxygen atom of another.
- **Front**: Condensation Reaction
  **Back**: A chemical reaction where two molecules join together with the removal of a water molecule.
- **Front**: Hydrolysis Reaction
  **Back**: A chemical reaction where a large molecule is broken down into smaller molecules with the addition of a water molecule.
- **Front**: Monosaccharide
  **Back**: A simple sugar (e.g., glucose, fructose, galactose).
- **Front**: Glycosidic Bond
  **Back**: A covalent bond formed between two monosaccharides by a condensation reaction.
- **Front**: Amylose vs Amylopectin
  **Back**: Amylose: unbranched chain of alpha-glucose (1,4 bonds); Amylopectin: branched chain of alpha-glucose (1,4 and 1,6 bonds).
- **Front**: Cellulose Structure
  **Back**: A polymer of beta-glucose molecules; alternate molecules are rotated 180 degrees, allowing for straight chains and hydrogen bonding between chains (forming microfibrils).
- **Front**: Triglyceride
  **Back**: A lipid formed from one glycerol molecule and three fatty acids joined by ester bonds.
- **Front**: Phospholipid
  **Back**: A lipid with two fatty acids and a phosphate group; has a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail.
- **Front**: Primary Structure
  **Back**: The unique sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.
- **Front**: Secondary Structure
  **Back**: The coiling or folding of the polypeptide chain into an alpha-helix or beta-pleated sheet, held by hydrogen bonds.
- **Front**: Tertiary Structure
  **Back**: The further folding of the polypeptide into a specific 3D shape, held by disulfide bridges, ionic bonds, hydrogen bonds, and hydrophobic interactions.
- **Front**: Quaternary Structure
  **Back**: The arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains into a functional protein (e.g., haemoglobin).

## [A LEVEL] NUCLEIC ACIDS & PROTEIN SYNTHESIS
- **Front**: Nucleotide Components
  **Back**: A pentose sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base.
- **Front**: DNA polymerase
  **Back**: An enzyme that catalyzes the formation of the phosphodiester backbone by joining nucleotides together during DNA replication.
- **Front**: Transcription
  **Back**: The process of copying a gene's DNA sequence into a complementary messenger RNA (mRNA) molecule.
- **Front**: Translation
  **Back**: The process where ribosomes decode mRNA to produce a specific sequence of amino acids (a polypeptide).
- **Front**: Codon
  **Back**: A sequence of three nucleotides on mRNA that codes for a specific amino acid.
- **Front**: Anticodon
  **Back**: A sequence of three nucleotides on tRNA that is complementary to a codon on mRNA.

## [A LEVEL] CELL TRANSPORT & MEMBRANES
- **Front**: Fluid Mosaic Model
  **Back**: Describes the cell membrane as a fluid phospholipid bilayer with various proteins "mosaic" embedded within it.
- **Front**: Facilitated Diffusion
  **Back**: The passive movement of molecules across a membrane through specific transport proteins (channel or carrier proteins).
- **Front**: Endocytosis
  **Back**: The bulk transport of substances into the cell by the infolding of the cell membrane to form a vesicle.
- **Front**: Exocytosis
  **Back**: The bulk transport of substances out of the cell by the fusion of a vesicle with the cell membrane.

## [A LEVEL] THE CELL CYCLE
- **Front**: Mitosis Phases
  **Back**: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
- **Front**: Cytokinesis
  **Back**: The division of the cytoplasm following nuclear division to form two separate daughter cells.
- **Front**: Stem Cell
  **Back**: An undifferentiated cell capable of dividing by mitosis and differentiating into specialized cell types.
- **Front**: Totipotent
  **Back**: Stem cells that can differentiate into any cell type, including embryonic and placental tissues.
- **Front**: Pluripotent
  **Back**: Stem cells that can differentiate into any body cell type (all embryonic tissues).
